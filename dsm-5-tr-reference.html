Neurodevelopmental Disorders

DSM-5-TR · onset in the developmental period

DSM-5-TR

Intellectual Developmental Disorder

also: Intellectual Disability

Deficits in intellectual functioning (reasoning, problem-solving, learning) and in adaptive functioning, beginning in the developmental period.

  • Confirmed by both clinical assessment and standardized testing
  • Adaptive deficits across conceptual, social, and practical domains limit independence
ThresholdOnset during the developmental period.
SubtypesSeverity (mild / moderate / severe / profound) is set by adaptive functioning, not IQ score alone.
DifferentialsGlobal developmental delay (used under age 5 when severity can't yet be reliably assessed); specific learning disorder; neurocognitive disorders (acquired, later decline).
NEAR / RELATED

Global Developmental Delay

Reserved for children under 5 who fail to meet expected developmental milestones across several areas but cannot yet be reliably assessed.

DifferentialsProvisional; re-evaluate as the child matures toward a specific diagnosis.
DSM-5-TR

Communication Disorders

A group covering difficulties with language, speech sounds, fluency, and social communication.

  • Language Disorder — persistent difficulty acquiring/using language across modalities
  • Speech Sound Disorder — difficulty with speech-sound production affecting intelligibility
  • Childhood-Onset Fluency Disorder (Stuttering) — disturbance in fluency and time patterning
  • Social (Pragmatic) Communication Disorder — difficulty with the social use of verbal/nonverbal communication
DifferentialsSocial communication disorder vs. autism: autism additionally requires restricted, repetitive behaviors/interests; if those are present, it is not SCD.
DSM-5-TR

Autism Spectrum Disorder

Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction across contexts, together with restricted, repetitive patterns of behavior, interests, or activities.

  • Social-communication deficits in reciprocity, nonverbal communication, and relationships
  • Restricted/repetitive behaviors: stereotypies, insistence on sameness, fixated interests, sensory hyper-/hyporeactivity (≥2 of 4)
  • Present in early development; may be masked until social demands exceed capacity
ThresholdSymptoms present in the early developmental period.
SubtypesSeverity levels 1–3 (support needs) specified separately for social communication and for restricted/repetitive behavior; with/without intellectual or language impairment.
DifferentialsSocial (pragmatic) communication disorder (no RRBs); intellectual disability; ADHD; language disorder; anxiety.
DSM-5-TR

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder

also: ADHD (“ADD” is the older term for the inattentive presentation)

A persistent pattern of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning or development.

  • Inattention: careless errors, poor sustained attention, not following through, disorganization, distractibility, forgetfulness
  • Hyperactivity-impulsivity: fidgeting, restlessness, talking excessively, interrupting, difficulty waiting
  • Enough symptoms from one or both lists (6 for children, 5 for age 17+)
ThresholdSeveral symptoms present before age 12, in ≥2 settings, causing impairment.
SubtypesPredominantly inattentive / predominantly hyperactive-impulsive / combined; severity mild-moderate-severe.
DifferentialsAge-appropriate activity; anxiety and depression (impair concentration); specific learning disorder; autism; oppositional defiant disorder; sensory problems.
DSM-5-TR

Specific Learning Disorder

Persistent difficulty learning and using academic skills (reading, written expression, or mathematics) well below age expectations.

  • Skills substantially below expected for age and cause impairment
  • Not explained by intellectual disability, sensory problems, or inadequate instruction
ThresholdDifficulties persist ≥6 months despite intervention; begin during school years.
SubtypesSpecify impairment in reading, written expression, and/or mathematics; severity.
DifferentialsIntellectual disability; ADHD; sensory deficits; lack of schooling/opportunity.
NEAR / RELATED

Tic Disorders (incl. Tourette's Disorder)

Sudden, rapid, recurrent motor and/or vocal tics with onset in childhood.

ThresholdTourette's requires both multiple motor and ≥1 vocal tic for >1 year.
DifferentialsPersistent (chronic) motor or vocal tic disorder; provisional tic disorder; stereotypic movement disorder.

Schizophrenia Spectrum & Other Psychotic Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Schizophrenia

A syndrome of active-phase psychotic symptoms with functional decline and continuous disturbance over an extended period.

  • Criterion-A symptoms: delusions, hallucinations, disorganized speech, grossly disorganized/catatonic behavior, negative symptoms
  • At least two present, and at least one must be delusions, hallucinations, or disorganized speech
  • Marked decline in work, relationships, or self-care
ThresholdContinuous signs ≥6 months, including ≥1 month of active-phase symptoms.
DifferentialsSchizophreniform (1–6 mo); brief psychotic (<1 mo); schizoaffective (concurrent mood episodes); mood disorder with psychotic features; substance-induced; medical cause.
DSM-5-TR

Schizophreniform Disorder

The same symptom picture as schizophrenia but of shorter duration and without a required decline in functioning.

ThresholdEpisode lasts 1–6 months.
DifferentialsBrief psychotic disorder (<1 month); schizophrenia (≥6 months, with functional decline).
DSM-5-TR

Brief Psychotic Disorder

Sudden onset of one or more positive psychotic symptoms with eventual full return to prior functioning.

Threshold≥1 day but <1 month.
SubtypesWith/without marked stressor; with peripartum onset.
DifferentialsSchizophreniform/schizophrenia (longer); substance-induced; mood disorder with psychosis.
DSM-5-TR

Schizoaffective Disorder

An illness in which a major mood episode occurs alongside schizophrenia-level psychosis, but psychosis also persists on its own.

  • A major mood episode (depressive or manic) is concurrent with Criterion-A psychosis
  • Delusions/hallucinations occur for ≥2 weeks without a mood episode at some point
  • Mood symptoms are present for the majority of the total illness
SubtypesBipolar type / depressive type.
DifferentialsSchizophrenia (mood episodes brief); mood disorder with psychotic features (psychosis only during mood episodes) — this is the key discriminator.
DSM-5-TR

Delusional Disorder

One or more delusions without the other hallmark features of schizophrenia, and with relatively preserved functioning apart from the delusion.

ThresholdDelusion(s) present ≥1 month.
SubtypesErotomanic, grandiose, jealous, persecutory, somatic, mixed, unspecified.
DifferentialsSchizophrenia (meets Criterion A more broadly); OCD/BDD with absent insight; paranoid personality disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Substance/Medication-Induced Psychotic Disorder

Prominent delusions or hallucinations arising during or soon after intoxication, withdrawal, or medication exposure capable of producing them.

DifferentialsPrimary psychotic disorder (psychosis persists well beyond substance effects); delirium.
DSM-5-TR

Psychotic Disorder Due to Another Medical Condition

Prominent psychotic symptoms judged to be the direct physiological consequence of a medical condition.

DifferentialsDelirium; substance-induced psychosis; primary psychotic disorder.

Bipolar & Related Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Manic Episode (building block)

A distinct period of abnormally elevated, expansive, or irritable mood plus increased energy or goal-directed activity.

  • Associated symptoms (DIGFAST): distractibility, indiscretion/risk, grandiosity, flight of ideas, activity increase, decreased need for sleep, talkativeness
  • ≥3 symptoms (≥4 if mood is only irritable)
Threshold≥1 week (or any duration if hospitalization is required).
DifferentialsCauses marked impairment, hospitalization, or psychosis — this severity distinguishes mania from hypomania.
DSM-5-TR

Hypomanic Episode (building block)

The same symptom cluster as mania but milder and briefer, representing an observable change without severe impairment.

Threshold≥4 consecutive days.
DifferentialsNo marked impairment, no hospitalization, and no psychotic features (any of these would make it mania).
DSM-5-TR

Bipolar I Disorder

Defined by at least one lifetime manic episode; depressive and hypomanic episodes commonly occur but are not required.

Threshold≥1 manic episode ever.
DifferentialsBipolar II (no full mania); schizoaffective (psychosis outside mood episodes); substance-induced; ADHD (chronic, not episodic).
DSM-5-TR

Bipolar II Disorder

A pattern of at least one hypomanic episode and at least one major depressive episode, with no history of full mania.

Threshold≥1 hypomanic episode + ≥1 major depressive episode.
DifferentialsBipolar I (mania present); major depressive disorder (hypomania missed if not asked about) — screening for hypomania is essential; cyclothymia (subthreshold).
DSM-5-TR

Cyclothymic Disorder

Chronic mood instability with numerous hypomanic-level and depressive-level symptoms that never reach full episode criteria.

Threshold≥2 years (≥1 year in youth); symptoms present at least half the time with no symptom-free stretch >2 months.
DifferentialsBipolar I/II (full episodes); borderline personality disorder (reactive, rapid shifts).
DSM-5-TR

Substance/Medication-Induced Bipolar Disorder

Mood elevation clearly tied to a substance or medication capable of producing it.

DifferentialsPrimary bipolar disorder (episodes independent of substances).

Depressive Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Major Depressive Episode (building block)

A two-week period of depressed mood or loss of interest/pleasure plus additional symptoms.

  • The nine symptoms: mood, anhedonia, weight/appetite change, sleep change, psychomotor change, fatigue, worthlessness/guilt, concentration, thoughts of death
  • ≥5 symptoms, at least one being depressed mood OR loss of interest/pleasure
ThresholdSame 2-week period, most of the day nearly every day.
DifferentialsBuilding block for MDD; also seen in bipolar disorders.
DSM-5-TR

Major Depressive Disorder

One or more major depressive episodes without any history of mania or hypomania.

Threshold≥2 weeks per episode; single or recurrent.
SubtypesSpecifiers: anxious distress, mixed, melancholic, atypical, psychotic, peripartum, seasonal.
DifferentialsBipolar disorders (screen for mania/hypomania before treating); persistent depressive disorder (≥2 years); adjustment disorder (does not meet full MDE); prolonged grief; medical/substance causes.
DSM-5-TR

Persistent Depressive Disorder

also: Dysthymia

Chronic, lower-grade depressed mood present more days than not over a long period.

  • Depressed mood plus ≥2 of: appetite change, sleep change, low energy, low self-esteem, poor concentration, hopelessness
Threshold≥2 years in adults (≥1 year in children/adolescents), without a symptom-free period >2 months.
DifferentialsMajor depressive disorder (episodic); the two can co-occur (“double depression”).
DSM-5-TR

Disruptive Mood Dysregulation Disorder

Chronic, severe irritability in children marked by frequent temper outbursts and a persistently angry mood between them.

  • Severe recurrent temper outbursts out of proportion to the situation
  • Persistently irritable/angry mood between outbursts
ThresholdOnset before age 10; diagnosed between ages 6 and 18; present ≥12 months.
DifferentialsOppositional defiant disorder (DMDD is more severe and mood-based); pediatric bipolar disorder (episodic); ADHD; anxiety.
DSM-5-TR

Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder

Mood and physical symptoms that recur in the final premenstrual week and remit after menses begins.

  • Mood lability, irritability, depressed mood, anxiety plus physical/behavioral symptoms
  • Confirmed by prospective daily ratings over ≥2 cycles
ThresholdLuteal-phase pattern that improves shortly after onset of menses.
DifferentialsPremenstrual syndrome (milder); exacerbation of another disorder premenstrually.
DSM-5-TR

Substance/Medication-Induced Depressive Disorder

Prominent depressed mood clearly linked to a substance or medication.

DifferentialsPrimary depressive disorder (persists beyond substance effects).
NEAR / RELATED

Prolonged Grief Disorder

Added in DSM-5-TR: intense, persistent grief that exceeds expected social/cultural norms and impairs functioning.

  • Persistent yearning/preoccupation with the deceased plus symptoms such as identity disruption, disbelief, avoidance, emotional pain, numbness, loneliness, meaninglessness
ThresholdDeath ≥12 months ago (≥6 months in children/adolescents); grief present most days ≥1 month.
DifferentialsNormative grief; major depressive disorder; PTSD.

Anxiety Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Separation Anxiety Disorder

Developmentally excessive fear or anxiety about separation from attachment figures.

Threshold≥4 weeks in children/adolescents; typically ≥6 months in adults.
DifferentialsGeneralized anxiety; agoraphobia; autism; illness anxiety.
DSM-5-TR

Selective Mutism

Consistent failure to speak in specific social situations where speech is expected, despite speaking elsewhere.

Threshold≥1 month (not limited to the first month of school).
DifferentialsSocial anxiety disorder; communication disorders; autism.
DSM-5-TR

Specific Phobia

Marked, out-of-proportion fear of a specific object or situation that is actively avoided.

Threshold≥6 months.
SubtypesAnimal, natural environment, blood-injection-injury, situational, other.
DifferentialsAgoraphobia; social anxiety; panic disorder; OCD.
DSM-5-TR

Social Anxiety Disorder

also: Social Phobia

Marked fear of social situations involving possible scrutiny, driven by fear of negative evaluation or embarrassment.

Threshold≥6 months.
SubtypesPerformance-only specifier.
DifferentialsPanic disorder; agoraphobia; autism; avoidant personality disorder; body dysmorphic disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Panic Disorder

Recurrent unexpected panic attacks followed by persistent worry about attacks or maladaptive change in behavior.

Threshold≥1 month of worry or behavioral change after an attack.
DifferentialsAgoraphobia; other anxiety disorders where attacks are expected/cued; medical causes (cardiac, thyroid).
NEAR / RELATED

Panic Attack (specifier)

An abrupt surge of intense fear or discomfort peaking within minutes, with physical and cognitive symptoms.

DifferentialsNot a codable disorder on its own; used as a specifier across many diagnoses.
DSM-5-TR

Agoraphobia

Marked fear or avoidance of two or more situations (transit, open spaces, enclosed spaces, crowds/lines, being outside the home alone) due to fear of not escaping or getting help.

Threshold≥6 months.
DifferentialsSpecific phobia (situational); social anxiety; panic disorder; PTSD.
DSM-5-TR

Generalized Anxiety Disorder

Excessive, hard-to-control worry about multiple domains, accompanied by physical tension symptoms.

  • ≥3 of 6 symptoms in adults (1 in children): restlessness, fatigue, poor concentration, irritability, muscle tension, sleep disturbance
ThresholdMore days than not for ≥6 months.
DifferentialsPanic disorder; social anxiety; OCD; illness anxiety; depression; medical/substance causes.

Obsessive-Compulsive & Related Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder

Recurrent obsessions (intrusive thoughts/urges/images) and/or compulsions (repetitive behaviors or mental acts) that are time-consuming or distressing.

ThresholdTypically >1 hour/day or causing marked distress/impairment.
SubtypesInsight specifier (good/fair, poor, absent); tic-related.
DifferentialsGeneralized anxiety (realistic worries); body dysmorphic disorder; hoarding; illness anxiety; OCPD (ego-syntonic traits, no true obsessions/compulsions).
DSM-5-TR

Body Dysmorphic Disorder

Preoccupation with one or more perceived defects in appearance that are not observable or appear slight to others, with repetitive checking or grooming.

  • Repetitive behaviors: mirror checking, grooming, reassurance-seeking, comparing
SubtypesWith muscle dysmorphia; insight specifier.
DifferentialsAnorexia/eating disorders (concern is weight/shape); OCD; social anxiety; gender dysphoria (distress about gender, not appearance defect).
DSM-5-TR

Hoarding Disorder

Persistent difficulty discarding possessions regardless of value, leading to accumulation that congests living spaces.

SubtypesWith excessive acquisition; insight specifier.
DifferentialsOCD; major neurocognitive disorder; depression; autism.
DSM-5-TR

Trichotillomania

also: Hair-Pulling Disorder

Recurrent pulling out of one's hair causing hair loss, with repeated attempts to stop.

DifferentialsOCD; body dysmorphic disorder; another medical/dermatologic cause; stereotypic movement disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Excoriation Disorder

also: Skin-Picking Disorder

Recurrent skin picking resulting in lesions, with repeated attempts to stop.

DifferentialsOCD; body dysmorphic disorder; nonsuicidal self-injury; dermatologic/substance causes.

Trauma- & Stressor-Related Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Reactive Attachment Disorder

A pattern of inhibited, emotionally withdrawn behavior toward caregivers in a child who experienced insufficient care.

ThresholdEvident before age 5; developmental age ≥9 months.
DifferentialsAutism; depression; intellectual disability; disinhibited social engagement disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Disinhibited Social Engagement Disorder

Overly familiar, culturally inappropriate approach to unfamiliar adults, arising from a history of insufficient care.

DifferentialsADHD; reactive attachment disorder (withdrawn rather than disinhibited).
DSM-5-TR

Posttraumatic Stress Disorder

A syndrome following exposure to actual or threatened death, serious injury, or sexual violence, spanning four symptom clusters.

  • Intrusion (memories, nightmares, flashbacks)
  • Avoidance of reminders
  • Negative alterations in cognition and mood
  • Alterations in arousal and reactivity (hypervigilance, startle, sleep, irritability)
ThresholdSymptoms persist >1 month.
SubtypesWith dissociative symptoms; with delayed expression; preschool subtype (≤6 years).
DifferentialsAcute stress disorder (<1 month); adjustment disorder; complex PTSD (ICD-11); depression; panic; dissociative disorders.
DSM-5-TR

Acute Stress Disorder

A trauma-response syndrome occurring in the first month, drawn from intrusion, negative mood, dissociation, avoidance, and arousal symptoms.

ThresholdFrom 3 days to 1 month after exposure.
DifferentialsPTSD (>1 month); adjustment disorder; normative acute reactions.
DSM-5-TR

Adjustment Disorders

Emotional or behavioral symptoms that develop in response to an identifiable stressor and exceed what would be expected or impair functioning.

ThresholdOnset within 3 months of the stressor; resolves within 6 months after the stressor (or its consequences) end.
SubtypesWith depressed mood, anxiety, mixed, conduct disturbance, or mixed emotions/conduct.
DifferentialsMajor depressive disorder / PTSD / anxiety disorders (if full criteria are met, diagnose those instead); normative stress reactions.
NEAR / RELATED

Complex PTSD

An ICD-11 diagnosis (not a separate DSM-5-TR category) for prolonged or repeated trauma: core PTSD plus disturbances in self-organization.

  • Core PTSD (re-experiencing, avoidance, threat sense) PLUS affect dysregulation, negative self-concept, and relationship disturbances
DifferentialsIn the DSM-5-TR this presentation is captured as PTSD (often with the dissociative subtype); borderline personality disorder (identity instability and abandonment fear vs. stable negative self-concept).

Dissociative Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Dissociative Identity Disorder

Disruption of identity involving two or more distinct personality states, with recurrent gaps in recall.

  • Marked discontinuity in sense of self and agency
  • Recurrent amnesia for everyday events, personal information, or trauma
DifferentialsPTSD; borderline personality disorder; psychotic disorders; malingering; seizure disorders.
DSM-5-TR

Dissociative Amnesia

Inability to recall important autobiographical information, usually of a traumatic or stressful nature, beyond ordinary forgetting.

SubtypesWith dissociative fugue.
DifferentialsNeurocognitive disorders; substance effects; PTSD; ordinary forgetting.
DSM-5-TR

Depersonalization/Derealization Disorder

Persistent or recurrent experiences of detachment from oneself (depersonalization) or from surroundings (derealization).

  • Reality testing remains intact throughout
DifferentialsPanic disorder; PTSD; psychotic disorders (reality testing lost); substance effects.

Somatic Symptom & Related Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Somatic Symptom Disorder

One or more distressing physical symptoms accompanied by excessive thoughts, feelings, or behaviors about them.

  • The symptom(s) may or may not have a clear medical explanation; the disproportionate response is the focus
ThresholdSymptomatic state typically >6 months.
DifferentialsIllness anxiety disorder (few/no somatic symptoms); factitious disorder (deliberate falsification); medical conditions; adjustment disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Illness Anxiety Disorder

Preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness when somatic symptoms are minimal or absent.

  • High health anxiety; excessive checking or maladaptive avoidance of care
Threshold≥6 months (the feared illness may change).
DifferentialsSomatic symptom disorder (prominent somatic symptoms); GAD; OCD; panic; adjustment disorder.
NEAR / RELATED

Functional Neurological Symptom Disorder

also: Conversion Disorder

Neurological symptoms (weakness, movement, sensory, or seizure-like) that are internally inconsistent with recognized neurological disease.

DifferentialsNeurological/medical disease; factitious disorder; malingering.
NEAR / RELATED

Factitious Disorder

Falsification of physical or psychological signs or symptoms, or induction of injury/disease, with deceptive presentation even absent external reward.

DifferentialsSomatic symptom disorder (symptoms not intentionally produced); malingering (external incentive present).

Feeding & Eating Disorders

DSM-5-TR

NEAR / RELATED

Pica

Persistent eating of non-nutritive, non-food substances that is developmentally inappropriate.

Threshold≥1 month.
DifferentialsOther eating disorders; intellectual disability/autism (may co-occur); medical causes.
NEAR / RELATED

Rumination Disorder

Repeated regurgitation of food that is re-chewed, re-swallowed, or spit out.

Threshold≥1 month.
DifferentialsGastrointestinal conditions; other eating disorders.
DSM-5-TR

Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder

also: ARFID

Restricted or avoidant eating leading to nutritional, weight, or psychosocial consequences, without weight/shape concerns.

DifferentialsAnorexia nervosa (ARFID lacks body-image disturbance and fear of weight gain); medical causes; autism-related selectivity.
DSM-5-TR

Anorexia Nervosa

Restriction of intake leading to significantly low body weight, intense fear of weight gain, and a disturbance in body-image/weight perception.

  • Persistent behaviors that interfere with weight gain
  • Undue influence of weight/shape on self-evaluation, or lack of recognition of the seriousness of low weight
SubtypesRestricting type vs. binge-eating/purging type; severity by BMI.
DifferentialsBulimia (weight not significantly low); body dysmorphic disorder (concern isn't weight/shape); ARFID; depression; medical causes. Low weight can be medically life-threatening.
DSM-5-TR

Bulimia Nervosa

Recurrent binge eating with recurrent inappropriate compensatory behaviors, and self-evaluation unduly influenced by shape and weight.

ThresholdOn average ≥1×/week for 3 months.
DifferentialsAnorexia, binge/purge type (occurs at significantly low weight); binge-eating disorder (no compensation).
DSM-5-TR

Binge-Eating Disorder

Recurrent binge eating with marked distress, without the regular compensatory behaviors of bulimia.

ThresholdOn average ≥1×/week for 3 months.
DifferentialsBulimia (compensation present); obesity without binge episodes; depression.

Elimination Disorders

DSM-5-TR

NEAR / RELATED

Enuresis

Repeated voiding of urine into bed or clothes, whether involuntary or intentional.

Threshold≥2×/week for 3 months or clinically significant distress/impairment; chronological age ≥5.
SubtypesNocturnal only / diurnal only / combined.
DifferentialsMedical causes (UTI, diabetes); medication effects.
NEAR / RELATED

Encopresis

Repeated passage of feces into inappropriate places.

Threshold≥1×/month for 3 months; chronological age ≥4.
SubtypesWith / without constipation and overflow incontinence.
DifferentialsMedical/gastrointestinal causes.

Disruptive, Impulse-Control & Conduct Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Oppositional Defiant Disorder

A pattern of angry/irritable mood, argumentative/defiant behavior, and vindictiveness, primarily toward authority figures.

  • ≥4 symptoms across the three groupings; vindictiveness requires ≥2 instances in 6 months
Threshold≥6 months.
DifferentialsConduct disorder (no rights violations in ODD); DMDD (persistent irritability + severe outbursts); ADHD; normative defiance.
DSM-5-TR

Intermittent Explosive Disorder

Recurrent impulsive aggressive outbursts grossly out of proportion to provocation and not premeditated.

ThresholdFrequent verbal/minor aggression, or fewer damaging/injurious outbursts, over specified periods; age ≥6.
DifferentialsConduct disorder / antisocial PD (planned, instrumental aggression); DMDD; substance effects.
DSM-5-TR

Conduct Disorder

A repetitive pattern violating the basic rights of others or major age-appropriate norms.

  • Categories: aggression to people/animals, destruction of property, deceitfulness/theft, serious rule violations
  • ≥3 behaviors in the past 12 months
SubtypesChildhood-onset / adolescent-onset / unspecified; with limited prosocial emotions.
DifferentialsOppositional defiant disorder (no serious rights violations); antisocial personality disorder (adults ≥18); adjustment disorder.
NEAR / RELATED

Kleptomania

Recurrent failure to resist impulses to steal objects not needed for use or value, with tension before and relief after.

DifferentialsOrdinary theft (for gain); conduct disorder/antisocial PD; manic episode.
NEAR / RELATED

Pyromania

Deliberate, purposeful fire-setting on more than one occasion, with fascination and tension/relief around fire.

DifferentialsArson for gain; conduct disorder; manic or psychotic states.

Substance-Related & Addictive Disorders

DSM-5-TR

DSM-5-TR

Substance Use Disorder

A problematic pattern of use leading to clinically significant impairment or distress, graded by number of criteria met.

  • Criteria span impaired control, social impairment, risky use, and pharmacological features (tolerance, withdrawal)
  • Applied per substance (alcohol, opioids, stimulants, cannabis, etc.)
Threshold≥2 of 11 criteria within a 12-month period.
SubtypesSeverity: mild (2–3), moderate (4–5), severe (6+); in early/sustained remission; on maintenance therapy.
DifferentialsNonproblematic use; medical/other mental disorders.
NEAR / RELATED

Tolerance & Withdrawal (concepts)

Tolerance = needing more for the same effect (or reduced effect at the same dose). Withdrawal = a substance-specific syndrome on reducing/stopping.

DifferentialsExpected physiological adaptation under appropriate medical use is weighed separately from a use disorder.
DSM-5-TR

Gambling Disorder

Persistent, problematic gambling behavior causing significant impairment or distress — the one behavioral addiction in this chapter.

Threshold≥4 of 9 criteria within 12 months.
DifferentialsProfessional/social gambling; manic episode; other impulse-control problems.

Neurocognitive Disorders

DSM-5-TR · acquired decline from a prior level

DSM-5-TR

Delirium

An acute, fluctuating disturbance in attention and awareness with additional cognitive changes, caused by a medical condition, substance, or multiple factors.

  • Develops over hours to days; severity fluctuates during the day
  • Attention and orientation are prominently affected
DifferentialsMajor/mild neurocognitive disorder (gradual, stable); primary psychotic or mood disorders. A medical emergency to work up. Classic mimic of “dementia” in older adults (e.g., UTI, dehydration, medications).
DSM-5-TR

Major Neurocognitive Disorder

also: Dementia

Significant decline in one or more cognitive domains that interferes with independence in everyday activities.

SubtypesSpecify etiology (Alzheimer's, vascular, Lewy body, frontotemporal, TBI, etc.); with/without behavioral disturbance; severity.
DifferentialsMild neurocognitive disorder (independence preserved); delirium (acute/fluctuating); depression (“pseudodementia”); normal aging.
DSM-5-TR

Mild Neurocognitive Disorder

Modest cognitive decline that does not yet interfere with independence, though greater effort or compensation may be needed.

DifferentialsMajor neurocognitive disorder (loss of independence); normal aging; delirium.

Personality Disorders

DSM-5-TR · enduring, pervasive, inflexible patterns

DSM-5-TR

General Personality Disorder

An enduring pattern of inner experience and behavior deviating markedly from cultural expectations, across cognition, affect, interpersonal functioning, and impulse control.

  • Pervasive and inflexible across situations
  • Stable and long-standing, traceable to adolescence or early adulthood
  • Leads to distress or impairment
DifferentialsDistinguish trait (enduring pattern) from state (episodic disorder). Rule out another mental disorder, substances, and medical conditions.
DSM-5-TR

Paranoid Personality Disorder

Pervasive distrust and suspiciousness; others' motives are read as malevolent (Cluster A).

DifferentialsDelusional disorder/schizophrenia (frank psychosis); PTSD; schizotypal/schizoid PD.
DSM-5-TR

Schizoid Personality Disorder

Detachment from social relationships and a restricted range of emotional expression (Cluster A).

DifferentialsAutism; schizotypal PD; avoidant PD (desires closeness but fears it).
DSM-5-TR

Schizotypal Personality Disorder

Acute discomfort with close relationships plus cognitive/perceptual distortions and eccentric behavior (Cluster A; also in the schizophrenia spectrum).

DifferentialsSchizophrenia (frank psychosis); autism; paranoid/schizoid PD.
DSM-5-TR

Antisocial Personality Disorder

A pervasive disregard for and violation of the rights of others (Cluster B).

ThresholdAge ≥18 with evidence of conduct disorder before age 15.
DifferentialsConduct disorder (under 18); narcissistic PD; substance use disorders; manic episodes.
DSM-5-TR

Borderline Personality Disorder

Pervasive instability of relationships, self-image, and affect, together with marked impulsivity (Cluster B).

  • ≥5 of 9: abandonment fears, unstable intense relationships, identity disturbance, impulsivity, suicidal/self-harm behavior, affective instability, emptiness, anger, transient paranoia/dissociation
DifferentialsBipolar disorder (sustained episodes vs. reactive shifts); complex PTSD; other Cluster B PDs; depression.
DSM-5-TR

Histrionic Personality Disorder

Pervasive excessive emotionality and attention-seeking (Cluster B).

DifferentialsBorderline/narcissistic PD; dependent PD.
DSM-5-TR

Narcissistic Personality Disorder

Pervasive grandiosity, need for admiration, and lack of empathy (Cluster B).

DifferentialsAntisocial/borderline/histrionic PD; manic episodes.
DSM-5-TR

Avoidant Personality Disorder

Pervasive social inhibition, feelings of inadequacy, and hypersensitivity to negative evaluation (Cluster C).

DifferentialsSocial anxiety disorder (substantial overlap); schizoid PD (avoidant desires closeness); dependent PD.
DSM-5-TR

Dependent Personality Disorder

A pervasive, excessive need to be taken care of, leading to submissive and clinging behavior (Cluster C).

DifferentialsBorderline/histrionic PD; avoidant PD; agoraphobia.
DSM-5-TR

Obsessive-Compulsive Personality Disorder

Pervasive preoccupation with orderliness, perfectionism, and control at the expense of flexibility and openness (Cluster C).

DifferentialsOCD (true obsessions/compulsions; OCPD traits are ego-syntonic); hoarding disorder; narcissistic PD.
