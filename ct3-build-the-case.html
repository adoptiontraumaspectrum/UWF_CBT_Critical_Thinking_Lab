Part B · Weigh two hypotheses

Two experienced clinicians formulate Maggie's maintaining mechanism differently. Neither is a trick answer — the skill is reasoning about which the evidence better supports, while staying open to both.

Hypothesis 1 — Inflated responsibility & control

Her anxiety is maintained mainly by a belief that safety depends on staying in control of everything, driving checking and over-preparation.

Hypothesis 2 — Intolerance of uncertainty

Her anxiety is maintained mainly by an inability to tolerate not knowing, driving avoidance of situations with unpredictable outcomes.

What good formulation reasoning looks like

Evidence rarely points cleanly to one answer. A strong clinician weighs the preponderance, names which data are ambiguous, holds more than one hypothesis when the case warrants it, and treats the formulation as a testable draft — revised as new evidence (and the client's response to intervention) comes in. Certainty is not the goal; a defensible, revisable model is.

This is a reasoning exercise, not a graded quiz. Nothing is submitted or stored.
